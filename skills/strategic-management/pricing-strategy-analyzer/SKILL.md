# Pricing Strategy Analyzer

> Price on evidence, not on gut feel or a competitor's pricing page.

## What this skill is

A structured pricing review that moves from what the company charges today to what it should charge and how to get there. It combines value-based pricing (Patrick Campbell, ProfitWell), usage-based pricing (Kyle Poyar, OpenView), outcome-based pricing (as used by AI support agents such as Intercom Fin and Zendesk), Good-Better-Best packaging, Marcos Rivera's Street Pricing, and Elena Verna's work on Product-Led Growth (PLG) monetization. The output is a recommendation backed by revenue scenarios and a migration plan.

## What it solves

- Pricing set by copying competitors, with no link to the value customers receive
- A value metric (per seat, per unit of usage) that grows slower than customer value
- Heavy, inconsistent discounting that signals the list price is wrong
- Too many tiers, or tiers whose feature gates do not trigger upgrades
- Price changes modeled only for new customers, ignoring churn and expansion in the installed base
- Migrations that surprise existing customers and the sales team

## When to invoke

- Win rates are falling on price, or deals close with little negotiation (money left on the table)
- Launching new tiers, add-ons, or a new product line
- Annual or post-funding pricing review
- Moving from seat-based to usage-based, outcome-based, or hybrid pricing
- Pricing an AI feature where inference cost scales with use

## Phase 1: Document current pricing

| Item | Capture |
|------|---------|
| Tiers and list prices | Every plan, add-on, and price point |
| Pricing model | Flat, per seat, usage, outcome, hybrid |
| Value metric | The unit the price scales with |
| Discounting | Frequency, average depth, who approves |
| Deal size | Average and median Annual Contract Value (ACV) by segment |
| Revenue mix | Share of revenue by tier and segment |

## Phase 2: Analyze competitive pricing

| Competitor | Model | Value metric | Entry price | Mid-tier price | Key features gated |
|------------|-------|--------------|------------:|---------------:|--------------------|
| [Competitor A] | | | $ | $ | |

Map features to price points, then place the company on a price-versus-value chart: premium, parity, or value positioning. Note where the company charges less than competitors for equal or greater value.

## Phase 3: Evaluate the pricing model and value metric

| Model | Fits when | Watch out for |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| Seat-based | Value scales with users | Seat compression as AI automates work |
| Usage-based | Value scales with consumption | Unpredictable bills, revenue volatility |
| Outcome-based | Outcomes are measurable and attributable | Attribution disputes, delayed revenue |
| Hybrid (platform fee plus usage) | Mixed value drivers | Complexity in quoting |

A good value metric is easy to understand, grows with customer value, and is predictable enough to budget for.

## Phase 4: Assess willingness to pay

Use the evidence available:
- **Win-loss data** - share of losses citing price, by segment
- **Discount frequency** - deals closing at list versus discounted
- **Upgrade and downgrade patterns** - which tier transitions happen and why
- **Customer research** - Van Westendorp price sensitivity or conjoint results where available

Segment the findings; enterprise and small business willingness to pay rarely match.

## Phase 5: Analyze packaging

| Check | Healthy sign |
|-------|--------------|
| Tier count | Three or four tiers (Good-Better-Best plus enterprise) |
| Feature gating | Each tier gates a feature tied to a clear upgrade trigger |
| Upgrade triggers | Usage limits or capabilities that customers hit as they grow |
| Expansion potential | Add-ons and usage growth create revenue without a new sale |
| Free or trial tier | Drives qualified conversion, not unlimited free usage |

## Phase 6: Model revenue impact

Build scenarios across the installed base and new business.

| Scenario | New customer conversion | Churn impact | Expansion impact | 12-month revenue | Change versus today |
|----------|------------------------:|-------------:|-----------------:|-----------------:|--------------------:|
| Status quo | | | | $ | - |
| Price increase | | | | $ | |
| Model change | | | | $ | |

State the assumption that most drives each scenario and the breakeven value at which it stops paying off.

## Phase 7: Plan the migration

- **Grandfathering** - who keeps current pricing, and for how long
- **Timeline** - announcement, effective date, and renewal-based transition
- **Communication** - customer notice, rationale, and value story
- **Sales enablement** - new price book, discount guardrails, objection handling
- **Risk mitigation** - churn watch list, escalation path, rollback criteria

## Output

- Current pricing baseline
- Competitive pricing map and positioning
- Pricing model and value metric recommendation
- Willingness-to-pay findings by segment
- Packaging recommendation with tier structure and gates
- Revenue scenario model with key assumptions
- Migration plan with timeline and communication

## Operating rules

**Always**
- Anchor price to customer value, then check it against competitors
- Model the effect on existing customers, not just new ones
- Segment willingness-to-pay evidence before drawing conclusions
- State the assumption behind every revenue scenario
- Plan the migration and sales enablement alongside the price change

**Never**
- Copy a competitor's price without understanding its value metric
- Treat discounting patterns as noise; they are pricing evidence
- Add tiers without a clear upgrade trigger for each
- Price an AI feature without accounting for inference cost
- Change pricing for existing customers without notice and a transition period
