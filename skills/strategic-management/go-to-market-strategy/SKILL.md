# Go-to-Market Strategy

> Most launches fail from poor go-to-market, not bad products.

## What this skill is

A workflow that turns "we are launching X" into a go-to-market (GTM) plan a team can execute. It clarifies the launch type and goals, prioritizes segments, maps channels and messages to each one, and sets a timeline and metrics that show within 30 days whether the launch is working. The result fits on one page, with a detailed plan behind it.

## What it solves

- 50-page launch decks nobody reads, or back-of-napkin plans that miss critical channels
- Launches aimed at "everyone" with no prioritized segment
- One generic message used for every persona
- Channels chosen by habit rather than where the segment actually discovers products
- Sales hearing about the launch on launch day, with no enablement
- Success judged only at 90 days, too late to correct course

## When to invoke

- Launching a new product, a major feature, or a new pricing tier
- Expanding into a new geography, industry, or customer segment
- Designing an enterprise sales play for a target account list
- Choosing or changing the primary go-to-market motion
- Reviewing a launch plan before committing budget

## Phase 1: Gather launch inputs

Ask for anything critical that is missing before planning.

| Input | Priority |
|-------|----------|
| What is launching (product, feature, expansion) | Critical |
| Target customers and segments | Critical |
| Launch date or window | Critical |
| Available budget and team | Nice to have |
| Competitive positioning | Nice to have |
| Current sales motion | Nice to have |

## Phase 2: Define launch type and goals

| Launch tier | Typical scope | Investment |
|-------------|---------------|------------|
| Tier 1 | New product or market entry | Full campaign, press, sales push |
| Tier 2 | Major feature or new segment | Targeted campaign and enablement |
| Tier 3 | Incremental improvement | Changelog, in-product messaging |

Set two or three measurable goals (for example, pipeline created, activated accounts, or revenue) with a target and a date.

## Phase 3: Prioritize target segments

Score each candidate segment 1-5:

| Segment | Benefit received | Reachability | Buying authority | Buying process simplicity | Total |
|---------|-----------------:|-------------:|-----------------:|--------------------------:|------:|
| [Segment A] | | | | | |

Pick one primary and at most two secondary segments. Name the segments deliberately left out.

## Phase 4: Choose the motion and map channels

Select the primary motion:

| Motion | Flow | Fits when |
|--------|------|-----------|
| Product-Led Growth (PLG) | Trial → conversion → expansion | Low price point, fast time to value |
| Sales-led | Outbound → demo → close | High contract value, complex buying |
| Marketing-led | Content → Marketing Qualified Lead (MQL) → Sales Qualified Lead (SQL) → close | Considered purchase, education needed |
| Channel-led | Partner → referral → close | Partners own the customer relationship |

Then map channels per segment:

|---------|------|---------|---------|-------------|
| [Economic buyer] | | | | |
| [Daily user] | | | | |
| [Technical evaluator] | | | | |

Every benefit needs a proof point: a metric, a customer result, or a demonstration.

## Phase 6: Build the timeline

| Window | Activities |
|--------|------------|
| Pre-launch (4-8 weeks) | Positioning sign-off, sales enablement, beta customers, content, press briefings |
| Launch day | Announcement, in-product messaging, sales outreach, partner notification |
| Post-launch (first 90 days) | Follow-up campaigns, customer stories, feedback loop, iteration |

Assign an owner and a date to every milestone.

## Phase 7: Define success metrics

| Horizon | Focus | Example metrics |
|---------|-------|-----------------|
| 30 days | Leading indicators | Sign-ups, demos booked, pipeline created |
| 60 days | Engagement | Activation rate, feature adoption, trial conversion |
| 90 days | Business outcomes | Revenue, win rate, retention of launch cohort |

Set a threshold for each metric that would trigger a course correction.

## Output

- GTM one-pager plus a detailed plan
- Launch tier and measurable goals
- Target segment prioritization
- Motion choice and channel strategy with owners
- Messaging matrix by persona
- Launch timeline with milestones and owners
- Sales enablement checklist
- 30/60/90-day success metrics with correction thresholds

## Operating rules

**Always**
- Prioritize one primary segment and name what is out of scope
- Match the motion to price point and buying complexity
- Tie every message to a persona and a proof point
- Enable sales before launch day
- Set leading indicators that show progress within 30 days

**Never**
- Target "everyone" or every channel at once
- Use one generic message for all personas
- Launch without an owner for each channel and milestone
- Wait until 90 days to judge whether the launch is working
- Treat go-to-market as a one-day event instead of a 90-day program
